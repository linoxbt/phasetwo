# Phase Two — Introduction Thread

Written for X (linked into Discord via the Neurocreative Contest submission flow). Educational framing — the trust problem, then how validator judgment works, with Phase Two as the example — rather than a raw build announcement.

**1/**
I want to tell you about a problem that has bothered me for years, and the thing I built to fix it.

**2/**
Every time you pay someone before the work is done, you're trusting them. Every time you work before you're paid, you're trusting the other person. That trust usually needs a third party sitting in the middle. A platform, an escrow agent, someone taking a cut just to vouch for both sides.

**3/**
I built Phase Two to remove that middleman entirely, and replace it with something stranger: independent AI validators who actually check the work themselves.

**4/**
Here's the idea. A depositor locks payment in a smart contract and writes exactly what they expect, in plain English. No legal jargon, just a real description of the deliverable.

**5/**
The counterparty does the work and submits proof. A repo, a live link, a document, whatever shows the job is done.

**6/**
Then several independent validators, running on GenLayer, fetch that evidence themselves and compare it against the spec. Not from a description someone typed. From the actual thing.

**7/**
If it holds up, payment releases on its own. If it doesn't, the depositor gets refunded. No arbitrator reading a support ticket three weeks later.

**8/**
I also built in the parts that matter for real work. Big projects can split into milestones instead of one giant payment. Every wallet carries a public track record, so you can see how someone has handled past engagements before you agree to work with them.

**9/**
Disputes exist too, but they're not free. Raising one costs a bond and only forces a re-check of the same evidence, so it can't be used to just stall the other side out.

**10/**
This is still early, and I'm actively building on it. If you want to see it, break it, or just tell me what's wrong with it, I'd genuinely rather hear that now than later. [link]
